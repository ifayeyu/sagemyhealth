Postpartum Hair Loss Screener

Losing alarming amounts of hair after having a baby can feel frightening — but up to 50% of new mothers experience it. This screener helps you understand where you are in the postpartum hair timeline, identify any factors making it worse, and know when to see a doctor.

Your timeline

Postpartum hair loss (telogen effluvium) typically begins 2–4 months after delivery and peaks around 3–6 months. Knowing your timeline is the most important piece of context.

Breastfeeding increases nutritional demands and sustains elevated prolactin, which can prolong or intensify shedding in some women.

Your shedding
How much hair are you losing?

During peak TE, losing 200–400+ hairs per day is common vs. the normal 50–100. It feels dramatic but is usually still within the expected range.

Where is the hair loss mainly occurring?

Typical postpartum TE is diffuse — evenly distributed all over. Patchy, spotty loss has a different cause and warrants medical attention.

Your health & wellbeing
Do you experience any of these beyond typical new-parent tiredness?

These include: constant cold hands or feet, unexplained weight gain or loss, persistent brain fog, heart palpitations, or feeling unusually anxious/depressed. These can signal postpartum thyroiditis, which affects 5–10% of new mothers and commonly accompanies hair loss.

Are you still taking prenatal or postnatal vitamins?
How are you coping with sleep deprivation and stress?

Chronic stress and sleep deprivation independently extend the shedding phase and can trigger additional rounds of telogen effluvium.

    What is postpartum hair loss?

    During pregnancy, high oestrogen levels keep far more hairs in the active growth phase than usual — which is why pregnancy hair often looks fuller and thicker. After delivery, oestrogen drops rapidly, triggering a mass shift of hair follicles into the resting (telogen) phase, followed by simultaneous shedding 2–4 months later.

    This is called telogen effluvium and it's not a sign that something is wrong — it's a normal hormonal reset. Most women regain their normal hair density by 12 months postpartum. However, nutritional deficiencies (especially iron) and thyroid changes can worsen or prolong the shedding, which is why identifying and addressing those factors matters.

    Understanding Postpartum Hair Loss — What's Normal and What's Not

    Postpartum hair shedding is one of the most common and most alarming experiences for new mothers, yet it is almost always a temporary and self-resolving physiological process rather than a sign of disease. During pregnancy, elevated oestrogen and progesterone levels prolong the anagen (growth) phase of the hair cycle, keeping hairs that would normally shed in active growth. After delivery, these hormones drop rapidly, allowing a large cohort of hairs to enter the telogen (resting) phase simultaneously. Two to four months later, these hairs shed — producing what can appear to be dramatic and frightening hair loss, particularly at the temples, crown, and frontal hairline. This is called telogen effluvium and affects up to 50% of postpartum women.

    Typical Postpartum Shedding
    Diffuse, beginning 2–4 months postpartum
    Characterised by diffuse, symmetric shedding, especially noticeable in the shower and on pillows. Typically peaks at 4 months and resolves by 6–12 months postpartum without intervention.
    Prolonged or Severe Shedding
    Continuing beyond 12 months postpartum
    Shedding beyond 12 months may indicate nutritional deficiency (especially iron, given breastfeeding demands), thyroid dysfunction triggered by delivery, or underlying androgenetic alopecia that was masked during pregnancy.
    Patchy Hair Loss
    Distinct bald patches emerging postpartum
    Patchy alopecia is not typical of postpartum telogen effluvium and suggests alopecia areata — an autoimmune condition that can be triggered by the immune shift postpartum. Dermatology evaluation is recommended.

    What to Do Next

    1. Check ferritin, thyroid function, and vitamin D postpartum. Breastfeeding dramatically increases iron and nutrient demands. Postpartum thyroiditis — an autoimmune thyroid condition — affects 5–10% of women after delivery and is a common and often missed cause of prolonged postpartum hair loss. Simple blood tests can identify both.
    2. Don't stop breastfeeding to address hair loss. Postpartum hair loss is not caused by breastfeeding. It is caused by the hormonal shift of delivery itself, which occurs regardless of feeding method. Continuing to breastfeed is associated with multiple maternal and infant health benefits; hair loss alone is not a clinical reason to stop.
    3. Maximise protein and iron intake while breastfeeding. Breastfeeding requires approximately 500 additional calories per day above pre-pregnancy needs. Protein and iron demands are also elevated. Ensuring adequate dietary intake — and confirming with ferritin testing — supports the fastest possible hair regrowth.
    4. Be patient — regrowth will be visible by 6–12 months in the typical case. The baby hairs you see regrowing at your hairline are a good sign. If shedding is ongoing and significant beyond 12 months, or if you notice the pattern is different from what you'd expect (patchy rather than diffuse), that is the time to seek dermatological evaluation.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Is postpartum hair loss permanent?

    In the typical case of telogen effluvium, no. Hair fully regrows to pre-pregnancy density in most women by 12 months postpartum. If there was underlying androgenetic alopecia before pregnancy that was simply masked by the pregnancy-induced growth phase, that progression may resume postpartum — in which case treatment is available.

    When does postpartum hair shedding peak?

    Most women notice the peak shedding between 3 and 5 months postpartum. The timing correlates with the lag between the hormonal trigger (delivery) and the completion of the telogen phase, which lasts approximately 2–4 months.

    Does breastfeeding make postpartum hair loss worse?

    Breastfeeding does not cause postpartum hair loss, but it does prolong the period of hormonal suppression (lower oestrogen) and increases nutritional demands — particularly for iron and protein — which can extend or worsen shedding if nutritional status is not adequately supported.

    What hair care practices help during postpartum shedding?

    Use a gentle, sulphate-free shampoo. Avoid tight hairstyles that add traction to fragile new growth. Limit heat styling. Scalp massage may modestly stimulate follicles. There is no topical product that has robust evidence for accelerating recovery from postpartum telogen effluvium — nutrition and time are the most important factors.

    Can postpartum hair loss happen multiple times?

    Yes, with each pregnancy. Women who experience significant postpartum shedding after one delivery will typically experience it again after subsequent deliveries. The pattern and severity are often similar each time, though not identical.
