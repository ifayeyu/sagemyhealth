    What is insulin resistance?

    Insulin resistance means your body's cells don't respond normally to insulin — the hormone that moves glucose from your blood into cells for energy. The pancreas compensates by producing more insulin, which can work for years before blood sugar begins to rise. By the time prediabetes is diagnosed, insulin resistance has often been present for a decade.

    It's estimated that up to 40% of US adults have insulin resistance, many without knowing it. The GLP-1 era has brought new awareness to the underlying metabolic dysfunction that drives obesity and type 2 diabetes — and insulin resistance is the central mechanism.

    Understanding Your HOMA-IR Insulin Resistance Score

    Insulin resistance — the condition in which cells throughout the body fail to respond efficiently to insulin's signalling — is one of the most consequential metabolic disorders of the modern era, estimated to affect 30–40% of adults in high-income countries. It is the metabolic root of type 2 diabetes, a major driver of cardiovascular risk, and strongly linked to polycystic ovary syndrome, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and certain cancers. The Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR), the formula used here, was developed at the University of Oxford in 1985 and remains one of the most validated and widely used clinical measures of insulin resistance despite requiring only two standard laboratory values: fasting glucose and fasting insulin.

    What Your Score Means

    Normal
    HOMA-IR < 1.0
    Insulin sensitivity appears healthy. Your pancreas is not having to work excessively to maintain blood glucose control.
    Borderline
    HOMA-IR 1.0–1.9
    Insulin sensitivity is mildly reduced. Lifestyle factors — particularly physical activity and dietary composition — are effective at this stage.
    Insulin Resistant
    HOMA-IR 2.0–2.9
    Meaningful insulin resistance is present. Pancreatic beta cells are working significantly harder to compensate. Medical evaluation and structured lifestyle intervention are indicated.
    Severely Insulin Resistant
    HOMA-IR ≥ 3.0
    Significant insulin resistance is present. This level is strongly associated with prediabetes, type 2 diabetes risk, and elevated cardiovascular risk. Clinical management is essential.

    What to Do Next

    1. Exercise is the most powerful non-pharmacological treatment for insulin resistance. Both aerobic exercise and resistance training independently improve insulin sensitivity — aerobic exercise by increasing glucose transporter expression (GLUT4) in muscle cells, and resistance training by increasing total muscle mass, which acts as the body's primary glucose storage organ.
    2. Reduce refined carbohydrate intake, especially ultra-processed foods. High glycaemic index foods drive repeated insulin spikes that over time blunt insulin receptor sensitivity. A low-glycaemic-index, higher-fibre diet consistently improves HOMA-IR in controlled trials, independent of caloric intake.
    3. Prioritise sleep quality — even one night of sleep deprivation measurably increases insulin resistance. In controlled studies, sleep restriction to 4–5 hours produces insulin resistance comparable to several months of high-fat diet. The mechanism involves elevated cortisol and disrupted growth hormone pulsatility.
    4. Work with your doctor to confirm and monitor. HOMA-IR requires fasting insulin, which is not in standard metabolic panels and must be specifically requested. Tracking HOMA-IR over time is one of the most direct ways to measure whether your lifestyle interventions are working metabolically.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is a normal HOMA-IR value?

    Reference ranges vary by population and laboratory. A commonly used threshold for insulin resistance is HOMA-IR above 2.0, though some researchers use 2.5 or 3.0. Values below 1.0 are generally considered to indicate good insulin sensitivity. Your ethnic background can affect the appropriate reference range.

    Is HOMA-IR the same as having diabetes?

    No. HOMA-IR measures insulin resistance — which is a metabolic state that precedes and predicts type 2 diabetes but is not itself a diagnosis of diabetes. Diabetes is diagnosed based on fasting glucose, HbA1c, or glucose tolerance testing.

    Can you be insulin resistant without being overweight?

    Yes. Insulin resistance occurs in people of all body weights, including those in the normal BMI range. This is sometimes called metabolically obese normal weight (MONW) or TOFI (thin outside, fat inside) and is associated with high visceral fat, low muscle mass, and poor lifestyle habits despite normal body weight.

    Does intermittent fasting help insulin resistance?

    Several trials show intermittent fasting (particularly time-restricted eating) improves insulin sensitivity, reduces fasting insulin, and lowers HOMA-IR. The mechanism is primarily a reduction in total caloric intake combined with extended periods of low insulin signalling. It is not uniquely superior to other dietary patterns that achieve the same caloric result.

    Which foods most directly worsen insulin resistance?

    The strongest evidence implicates: high-fructose corn syrup and added sugars (especially liquid sugar), refined grains, ultra-processed foods with low fibre, and trans fats. Saturated fat also impairs insulin signalling at high levels. Specific foods associated with better insulin sensitivity include legumes, whole grains, olive oil, fatty fish, and leafy greens.
