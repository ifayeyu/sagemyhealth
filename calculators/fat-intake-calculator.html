    How to Set Your Fat Intake

    Dietary fat spent decades as the villain of nutrition, and we're still recovering from the oversimplification. Here's the current, better-evidenced picture: fat is essential, the total amount within a wide band matters less than most people think, and the type of fat is what really moves health outcomes. Fat carries the fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K, supplies the essential fatty acids your body can't manufacture, supports hormone production, and makes food satisfying. This calculator sets your total using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation for calories and your chosen share within the 20–35% range, then gives you a saturated-fat ceiling to steer the quality.

    Total Fat vs. Type of Fat

    Large diet trials have repeatedly shown that healthy eating patterns exist across a wide range of fat intakes — from moderate-fat to the higher-fat Mediterranean pattern (around 35–40% of calories). What consistently helps is replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fat, and replacing trans fat with anything else. So rather than chasing a very low number, aim for a comfortable total and spend your fat budget mostly on olive oil, nuts, seeds, avocado, and fish.

    Getting the Quality Right

    1. Make unsaturated fats the default. Cook with olive or canola oil, snack on nuts, add avocado and seeds. These should make up the majority of your fat.
    2. Cap saturated fat. Keep it under the limit shown above (10% of calories; lower if you're managing cholesterol). The move that matters is replacing it with unsaturated fat.
    3. Eat fish twice a week. Oily fish supplies omega-3s (EPA and DHA) linked to heart and brain health; walnuts and flax offer a plant source.
    4. Minimise trans fats. Largely phased out of the food supply, but check labels on some fried and packaged foods for "partially hydrogenated oils."

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How much fat should I eat per day?
    About 20–35% of calories — roughly 44–78 g on a 2,000-calorie diet. Within that, most should be unsaturated, with saturated fat under 10% of calories. Use the calculator above for your personal number.
    How much saturated fat is too much?
    Keep it under 10% of daily calories (about 22 g on 2,000 calories); the AHA suggests ~6% for lowering cholesterol. Replacing saturated with unsaturated fat is what delivers the benefit.
    Is a higher-fat or low-fat diet healthier?
    Type matters more than total. Both moderate-fat and higher-fat patterns (like the Mediterranean diet) can be very healthy when the fat is mostly unsaturated. Very low-fat diets aren't necessary and can make eating less satisfying.
    Do I need dietary fat?
    Yes — it carries fat-soluble vitamins, provides essential fatty acids, supports hormones, and aids fullness. That's why a floor of about 20% of calories is recommended.
