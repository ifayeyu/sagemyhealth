What to do next:

    What is Energy Availability?

    Energy availability (EA) is the energy left over for your body's essential functions — hormones, bone remodelling, immunity, repair — after the fuel burned in exercise is subtracted, scaled to your fat-free (lean) mass:

    EA = (Intake − Exercise kcal) ÷ Fat-Free Mass (kg)

    Sports-medicine research places the healthy threshold at about 45 kcal/kg/day. Sustained EA below 30 kcal/kg/day is low energy availability — the root cause of RED-S, which harms bone, hormones, mood, immunity, and ultimately performance.

    Warning signs of under-fuelling

    Energy availability is a screening estimate. These symptoms are often the truer signal — if you have several, take it seriously regardless of the number:

    Missed or irregular periods
    Stress fractures or bone injuries
    Persistent fatigue & poor recovery
    Frequent illness or infections
    Low mood, irritability, poor sleep
    Stalled performance despite training

    Are You Eating Enough to Support Your Training?

    One of the most common — and most missed — problems in active people isn't overtraining; it's under-eating relative to how much they train. The body has a fixed set of essential jobs to fund: keeping hormones running, remodelling bone, fighting infection, repairing tissue. Exercise is an additional withdrawal from the same account. When intake doesn't cover both, the body quietly cuts spending on the "non-essential" functions — reproduction first, then bone, immunity, and metabolism. This is low energy availability, and its downstream syndrome is called RED-S. Crucially, it can happen at any body weight, in any sport, and in men as well as women.

    What Your Result Means

    Optimal (45+)
    You're fuelling both your training and your physiology well. Keep intake matched to training as your load changes through the season.
    Reduced (30–45)
    A grey zone. Short spells here are usually fine, but sustained time can start to affect hormones and recovery. Nudge intake up, especially around training.
    Low (<30)
    Below the threshold linked to RED-S. If sustained, this risks bone, hormonal, and metabolic health. Prioritise eating more and consider professional support — especially with any warning signs.

    If You're Under-Fuelling

    1. Eat more — start with carbohydrate. Most under-fuelled athletes are under on total energy and carbs specifically. Add substantial snacks around training first; it's the fastest, safest lever.
    2. Fuel around sessions. Don't train fasted repeatedly. Eat before, and get carbohydrate and protein in soon after hard sessions to aid recovery.
    3. Consider training load. If eating more isn't feasible quickly, temporarily easing volume closes the gap from the other side.
    4. Don't ignore the symptoms. Missed periods are never a normal consequence of training — they're a red flag. So are recurrent stress fractures and constant illness.
    5. Get help for low EA. RED-S affects hormones and bone, so low energy availability with symptoms warrants a sports physician and a sports dietitian, not self-management.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is a good energy availability?
    Around 45 kcal per kg of fat-free mass per day or more is considered optimal for health and performance. Below 30 is low energy availability, the threshold linked to RED-S. The 30–45 band is a grey zone that's usually fine short-term but risky if sustained.
    Can men get RED-S?
    Yes. RED-S replaced the older, female-only "athlete triad" concept precisely because low energy availability affects men too — lowering testosterone, harming bone, and impairing performance. It's underdiagnosed in male athletes.
    Why haven't I lost weight if I'm in a deficit?
    Sustained under-fuelling makes the body defend its weight by slowing metabolism and hormone output. Weight can hold steady while health quietly suffers, which is why symptoms matter more than the scale.
    How accurate is this estimate?
    It's a screening estimate only — self-reported intake and exercise energy are both hard to measure, and body-fat is estimated if you don't enter it. Use it to spot a likely problem and start a conversation, not as a diagnosis.
