    What is ASCVD risk?

    Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) includes heart attacks and strokes caused by plaque build-up in the arteries. The Pooled Cohort Equations (ACC/AHA 2013) estimate your 10-year risk of a first major event — used in real clinical practice to guide treatment decisions.

    Heart Age translates your risk score into something intuitive: the age at which a person with ideal risk factors would have the same 10-year risk. If your heart age is older than you are, your risk factors have accelerated cardiovascular ageing.

    Understanding your risk tier

    Low (<5%) — Your 10-year risk is reassuringly low. Focus on maintaining healthy habits. Statin therapy is not generally recommended.

    Borderline (5–7.4%) — Risk is slightly elevated. Lifestyle factors and a clinician conversation about individual risk can help clarify next steps.

    Intermediate (7.5–19.9%) — Moderate risk where statin therapy may be beneficial. A risk-benefit discussion with your doctor is recommended.

    High (≥20%) — High risk where statin therapy is strongly recommended alongside lifestyle changes and close monitoring.

    Understanding Your 10-Year Cardiovascular Risk Score

    The Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease (ASCVD) Pooled Cohort Equations, developed collaboratively by the American Heart Association and American College of Cardiology in 2013, represent the most widely used validated tool for estimating an individual's 10-year risk of a first major cardiovascular event — specifically heart attack or stroke. The model draws on data from multiple large cohort studies and incorporates age, sex, race, total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, systolic blood pressure, blood pressure treatment status, smoking, and diabetes. The resulting percentage guides shared decision-making about the threshold at which statin therapy, aspirin, and blood pressure medications offer a net benefit. This is not a prediction of certainty — it is a probability estimate that helps contextualise your risk and the potential benefit of intervention.

    What Your Score Means

    Low Risk
    10-year ASCVD <5%
    Low likelihood of a cardiovascular event in the next 10 years. Lifestyle optimisation remains the focus; pharmacological primary prevention is generally not recommended.
    Borderline Risk
    10-year ASCVD 5–7.4%
    Risk-enhancing factors (family history, inflammation, coronary calcium score) may tip the statin decision. Lifestyle intervention is the primary management approach.
    Intermediate Risk
    10-year ASCVD 7.5–19.9%
    This range triggers a clinician-patient discussion about statin therapy, where the ACC/AHA guidelines indicate a meaningful expected benefit from treatment. Lifestyle intervention is essential regardless of medication decision.
    High Risk
    10-year ASCVD ≥20%
    High-intensity statin therapy is recommended by ACC/AHA guidelines unless contraindicated. Multiple lifestyle and pharmacological interventions should be considered in a comprehensive risk reduction plan.

    What to Do Next

    1. Use this score as a conversation starter with your doctor, not a final answer. The Pooled Cohort Equations have known limitations — they may overestimate risk in contemporary populations with better risk factor control. A coronary artery calcium (CAC) score (a CT scan measure of arterial calcification) is a powerful additional test for the borderline risk range.
    2. Target the modifiable risk factors in your equation. LDL cholesterol, systolic blood pressure, smoking status, and diabetes are all modifiable inputs in your ASCVD score. Improving each produces a calculable, direct reduction in your 10-year probability.
    3. Never smoke or stop smoking now — the single highest-value cardiovascular intervention. Smoking approximately doubles cardiovascular risk at every age. Within 1 year of cessation, cardiovascular risk drops substantially; within 15 years, it approaches that of a never-smoker.
    4. Discuss the statin decision explicitly with your doctor using your ASCVD score. For intermediate-risk individuals, the ACC/AHA now recommends a shared decision conversation that includes the patient's values, risk-enhancing factors, and the expected absolute risk reduction from treatment before prescribing.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Does my ASCVD score mean I will have a heart attack?

    No. A 10% ASCVD score means that in a group of 100 people with your risk profile, approximately 10 are expected to have a first major cardiovascular event in the next decade. For any individual, the event either happens or doesn't — the percentage describes group probability, not individual destiny.

    What is a coronary artery calcium (CAC) score and should I get one?

    A CAC score is a CT scan of the coronary arteries that directly quantifies calcified plaque — the residue of atherosclerosis. It is the strongest single predictor of cardiovascular events beyond traditional risk factors and is particularly useful in the borderline-risk range to determine whether statin therapy is warranted. A CAC score of zero indicates very low near-term risk even in intermediate-risk individuals.

    Is the ASCVD calculator accurate for all ethnicities?

    The Pooled Cohort Equations were developed primarily from cohorts of White and Black Americans. Their accuracy in Hispanic, Asian, and South Asian populations is less well validated, and they may underestimate risk in some groups (particularly South Asians, who have elevated cardiovascular risk at younger ages and lower LDL levels than traditional risk calculators suggest). Ethnicity-specific calculators are increasingly available.

    Can a young person have a high ASCVD score?

    Age is the dominant driver of the ASCVD equation, so high scores in people under 40 are unusual using this specific tool. However, the absence of a high 10-year score does not mean cardiovascular health is optimal. Lifetime risk calculators and the coronary artery calcium score are more informative for young people with multiple risk factors.

    Does taking a statin definitely reduce my risk?

    Statin therapy consistently reduces LDL cholesterol by 30–50% and reduces cardiovascular events by a predictable proportion of baseline risk — the higher your baseline risk, the greater the absolute event reduction. In the intermediate-to-high risk range, statin therapy produces benefits that generally outweigh risks for most individuals.
