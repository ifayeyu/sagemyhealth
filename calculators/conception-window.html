Tips for maximising your chances:

  • Have sex every 1–2 days throughout your fertile window — not just on ovulation day. Sperm can survive 3–5 days and will be waiting for the egg when it's released.
  • Ovulation day itself and the day before are your two highest-probability days. Don't skip these even if you've had sex earlier in the window.
  • Ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) detect the LH surge 24–36 hours before ovulation — use them from fertile window day 1 to confirm timing, especially with irregular cycles.
  • Basal body temperature (BBT) tracking confirms ovulation has occurred (temperature rises 0.2°C after ovulation). It can help identify your personal pattern over several cycles.
  • Fertile cervical mucus (clear, stretchy, egg-white consistency) is a reliable physical sign that you're in your fertile window. It helps sperm travel to the egg.

The science of your fertile window

Each month, one of your ovaries releases a mature egg — a process called ovulation. The egg is viable for just 12–24 hours. However, because sperm can survive 3–5 days in the female reproductive tract, the fertile window extends 5 days before ovulation plus ovulation day itself — six days total.

Ovulation doesn't happen on a fixed calendar date; it happens a consistent number of days before your next period. This is the luteal phase — the time from ovulation to menstruation — which is typically 14 days and relatively stable. This is why the fertile window is calculated by counting backwards from when you expect your next period, not forwards from your last one.

For women with irregular cycles, the fertile window is harder to predict by date alone — this is where physical signs (cervical mucus, basal body temperature, OPK tests) become especially valuable.

Understanding Your Conception Window

Human fertility is tightly concentrated around the time of ovulation — a window of roughly 6 days per menstrual cycle during which conception is biologically possible. This is because sperm can survive in the female reproductive tract for up to 5 days under optimal conditions, while a mature egg is viable for only 12–24 hours after ovulation. The combined result is that timing intercourse to the days immediately before and including ovulation maximises the probability of conception in any given cycle. This calculator uses your last menstrual period (LMP) and typical cycle length to estimate your most likely fertile window, based on the standard clinical model established by Ogino-Knaus.

What to Do Next

  1. Track multiple fertility signs for better accuracy. Calendar calculation alone has significant error. Combining it with LH surge testing (ovulation predictor kits), basal body temperature (BBT) tracking, and cervical mucus changes gives a far more personalised and accurate picture of your actual fertile window.
  2. Prioritise intercourse in the 2–3 days leading up to estimated ovulation. The research by Wilcox et al. in the New England Journal of Medicine (1995) established that the highest pregnancy probability comes from intercourse in the days before ovulation — not after. The egg's 12–24 hour viability makes post-ovulation intercourse much less effective.
  3. If you've been trying for 12 months without success (6 months if over 35), see a specialist. One in six couples experience difficulty conceiving. Fertility evaluation in both partners is recommended after these timeframes — and earlier if there are known risk factors such as irregular cycles, prior pelvic infections, or endometriosis.
  4. Optimise pre-conception health in both partners. Male factor is involved in approximately 40–50% of infertility cases. For both partners: avoid smoking and excessive alcohol, maintain a healthy weight, start prenatal folate supplementation, manage chronic health conditions, and review all medications with your provider.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is calendar-based fertile window prediction?

Calendar methods assume regularity in cycle length and ovulation timing. In women with regular 28-day cycles, accuracy is reasonable. In women with irregular cycles, the calendar method can be significantly off. LH testing (ovulation predictor kits) directly detects the hormonal surge that triggers ovulation and is substantially more accurate.

Can you conceive outside the fertile window?

Theoretically possible but highly improbable. Sperm viability beyond 5 days is minimal, and egg viability beyond 24 hours is essentially zero. Conception outside the biologically defined fertile window accounts for a very small fraction of pregnancies.

Does stress affect fertility?

Chronic high stress is associated with menstrual irregularity and altered LH surge timing through cortisol's effects on the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis. The evidence that stress directly causes infertility in otherwise reproductively healthy individuals is less clear, but it can make cycle prediction less reliable.

What is the difference between this and the rhythm method?

The rhythm method is a contraception approach that uses cycle history to identify and avoid fertile days. Conception window tracking uses the same biological principles but with the opposite goal: identifying when to time intercourse to maximise conception probability. Both rely on cycle regularity.

Does a longer cycle mean a later ovulation?

Generally, yes. In most women, the luteal phase (from ovulation to menstruation) is relatively fixed at 12–14 days. Variation in cycle length is primarily driven by variation in the follicular phase (from menstruation to ovulation). A 35-day cycle typically means ovulation occurs around day 21, not day 14.
